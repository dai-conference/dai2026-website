  • A-GRAG: An Agentic GraphRAG Framework with Collaborative Agents for Temporal-Aware Multi-Hop Reasoning

    Haowei Mo, Fangling Leng, Yubin Bao, Tiancheng Zhang

    Abstract

    Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) mitigates hallucinations and outdated knowledge in Large Language Models (LLMs) by incorporating external knowledge. As a structured extension of RAG, GraphRAG organizes entities and relations from documents into a knowledge graph, thereby enhancing the ability of LLMs to reason over multi-hop queries. However, existing GraphRAG methods typically rely on one-shot retrieval and struggle to dynamically adjust retrieval targets during reasoning, while existing agentic retrieval methods rarely model knowledge graph structure and temporal constraints jointly. To address these limitations, we propose Agentic GraphRAG (A-GRAG), a framework that combines a temporally aware knowledge graph with agentic iterative retrieval. A-GRAG consists of a Temporal Extraction Agent (TEA) and an Iterative Retrieval Agent (IRA). During graph construction, TEA extracts and normalizes temporal information from entity descriptions and writes it to the corresponding entities as explicit attributes. During querying, TEA extracts temporal constraints from the current query and attempts to locate matching anchor entities using these constraints; if no match is found, the system falls back to the standard graph-retrieval procedure. IRA acquires candidate evidence, assesses whether it is sufficient to answer the original query, and, when the evidence is insufficient, identifies information gaps, compresses the auxiliary context, and generates a subquery for the next round, with at most three retrieval rounds. Experiments on three multi-hop question answering benchmarks show that A-GRAG achieves the best results in both EM and F1. Ablation results further show that, compared with the variant without temporal awareness, the full A-GRAG model maintains slightly better answer quality while reducing the average token consumption per question by 23.7%--30.9% and average end-to-end latency by 12.8%--17.8%.

  • Accelerating Meta-Reinforcement Learning with Predictive Curriculum Scheduling

    Fangjie Yang, Jing Yang, Yun Qu, Xingxing Liang, Guanlin Wu, Qi Wang, Longfei Zhang

    Abstract

    Meta-reinforcement learning (Meta-RL) enables rapid adaptation by leveraging shared structure across tasks, but suffers from poor sample efficiency due to costly meta-training and inefficient task sampling. In particular, uniform task sampling ignores the agent's evolving competence, wasting interaction on tasks that are either trivial or currently unsolvable. While curriculum learning offers a principled alternative, existing methods either rely on expensive online difficulty estimation or employ amortized predictors whose absolute difficulty estimates tend to drift as the learner improves, leaving them misaligned with the learner's competence. We propose Predictive Curriculum Scheduling (PCS), an adaptive framework that unifies amortized difficulty prediction with competence-aware curriculum design. PCS learns a predictive model of relative task difficulty with a rank-preserving objective, encouraging the task ordering used for scheduling to be less sensitive to shifts in absolute difficulty values during training. It further introduces a scheduling mechanism that dynamically tracks the agent's competence and prioritizes tasks just beyond its current mastery, enabling fully automated curriculum generation without additional environment interaction. Experiments on standard Meta-RL benchmarks demonstrate that PCS consistently improves sample efficiency, accelerates convergence, and enhances training stability over strong adaptive task-sampling baselines.

  • ADRO: Adaptive Dual-Timescale Response Optimization for Transfer Reinforcement Learning across Diverse Opponent Styles

    Daojie Guo, Yixing Lan, Tenglong Liu, Long Cheng, Xuhui Xie, Xin Xu

    Abstract

    Policies trained against a finite set of opponents can lose performance when opponent competence, action preferences, or strategy-witching patterns change. Opponent-modeling methods infer behavior from interaction history, while policy-reuse methods select or combine learned source policies. Adapting to a changing opponent requires balancing evidence accumulated over longer interactions with recent observations. We propose Adaptive Dual-Timescale Response Optimization (ADRO). ADRO uses a shared history-conditioned model to construct a long-term reference response policy and a short-term candidate response policy from long and recent interaction windows, respectively. The centered log-probability difference between the two policies defines a state-dependent exponential-family path. Dual-critic directional evidence and Fisher curvature determine the nominal transfer coordinate, while a forward Kullback–Leibler (KL) constraint limits departure from the reference policy. Across nine competitive tasks and one cooperative task, ADRO achieves an aggregate out-of-distribution(OOD) task-outcome interquartile mean of 86.3%, compared with 72.2% for the strongest evaluated baseline. The aggregate combines competitive win rates with relative-return success in the cooperative task under predefined parameterized opponent shifts.

  • Agentic Code Repair via Execution-Grounded Selective Planning

    Wei Wei, Teng Wang, Lin Li, Lijun Zhang

    Abstract

    Large language models can repair programs, but their outputs may still contain errors involving boundary conditions, interfaces, runtime behavior, or algorithmic logic. Existing approaches use execution feedback and planning to improve repair accuracy. However, repeated revisions increase inference cost, and limited visible tests may not provide sufficient evidence to justify replacing a partially correct program. To address these challenges, we propose Execution-Grounded Selective Repair (ESR), a centralized agentic framework that separates planning allocation from program updates under a bounded inference budget. ESR first generates direct repair candidates and evaluates them on visible tests. If no candidate passes all visible tests, the Planning Agent analyzes execution records from the complete candidate pool to identify recurring failure patterns. The resulting Failure Sketch, Repair Plan, and preservation constraints guide a subsequent Plan-Guided Repair stage. In both stages, PublicSelect ranks candidates by the number of visible tests they pass, while Guarded Update accepts a replacement only if it passes strictly more visible tests than the incumbent program. Experiments on TACO under matched generation budgets show that ESR outperforms additional direct sampling with both backbones and the evaluated PaT control with Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct. Across four Python benchmarks and two code backbones, ESR achieves the highest observed point estimate of Final Pass@1 in all eight combinations of backbone and benchmark. Selective planning reduces the number of model generations compared with a strategy that always executes both repair stages.

  • AGES: Audit-Gated Evaluation Scheduler for Combinatorial LLM Context Evaluation

    Zhu Weilin, Mu Weimin, Fan Liu, Jian Chen, ChengHanchang, Weiping Wang, Yun Zhang

    Abstract

    AI systems, including LLM-based agents, often construct prompts from retrieved memories or few-shot demonstrations. Measuring how individual context items and their combinations affect performance supports regression testing, debugging, and attribution. For n candidate items, a complete analysis may require scoring all 2^n subsets. Existing methods pursue related but different objectives. Selection methods return only a small number of subsets, while reconstruction methods estimate missing scores under structural assumptions. Compression methods identify repeated information after the scores are available. These approaches do not directly address whether an observed score can replace a new evaluation while the score table is being acquired. We study this question using frozen few-shot demonstrations in a controlled, single-turn code-generation setting. We introduce the Audit-Gated Evaluation Scheduler (AGES) to reduce this cost. AGES uses score agreement on small subsets to propose reuse, then audits the proposal on larger subsets before copying any score. If the audit finds a mismatch, AGES retains all observed scores and evaluates every remaining subset. The fallback completes the table exactly without discarding previous evaluations. We evaluate AGES through offline replays of score tables obtained with deterministic Qwen2.5-Coder generation. With a shared demonstration pool, the frozen procedure reduces simulated unique score reads by 4.2% on LeetCodeDataset and 16.2% on 588 query-disjoint MBPP+ model–query score tables, compared with exhaustive evaluation. On MBPP+, 0.026% of copied scores are incorrect, and the selected subset attains a true maximum in all 588 tables.

  • Aligning Context and Formulas: Multi-Stage Fine-Tuning for Large Language Models with A Novel Dataset ContextFormulas50K

    Xinyu Yin, Han Xiao

    Abstract

    Mathematics plays a crucial role in scientific research. However, it is never easy to formulate a problem using mathematical formulas, even for senior researchers. Fine-tuning pre-trained models on mathematical datasets to facilitate research has become a widely adopted approach. Although there are numerous mathematical datasets, only high-quality datasets could enhance the pre-trained model for a deep insight into formulas because mathematics is a precise discipline. To address this, we propose the ContextFormulas50K dataset, which consists of mathematical formulas paired with their contextual text. Based on this dataset, we fine-tune two pre-trained models (i.e. LLaMA3-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B) to generate precise formulas for research work. But in this task, there emerges the information bottleneck problem, which means the parameter scale expands much faster than performance. To overcome this problem, we introduce a Multi-Stage Fine-Tuning(MSFT) approach to equip large language models (LLMs) with better mathematical comprehension. Specifically, there are three stages in our model, and by progressively inserting plug-and-play modules, model performance could be enhanced at each stage, which means our method effectively alleviates information bottlenecks. Experimental results show that our model effectively improves mathematical comprehension and achieves state-of-the-art performance in the formula generation task, outperforming multiple commercial baselines (i.e., GPT-4, GPT-4o, and Claude-3.5-sonnet).

  • Applicability-Aware Evidence Reasoning for Knowledge Graph-Enabled Agents with Proposition Graphs

    Hejin Fang, Huihui Shao, Fanyu Wang, Zhenping Xie

    Abstract

    LLM-based agents increasingly interact with knowledge graphs (KGs) to acquire structured external knowledge for knowledge-intensive decision-making. However, reliable decisions require more than retrieving relevant entities or multi-hop paths: agents must also determine whether the acquired evidence is applicable under the current context, identify its prerequisites, resolve conflicts, and organize coherent support. Existing KG-enabled agents largely leave this evidence-reasoning stage to the implicit reasoning of the LLM, making them vulnerable to evidence that is factually plausible but conditionally valid, mutually conflicting, or contextually inappropriate. We propose PG-LLM, a plug-and-play applicability-aware evidence-reasoning module for KG-enabled agents. PG-LLM augments the evidence-reasoning process with a Proposition Graph (PG), a propositional reasoning substrate whose nodes represent self-contained propositions and whose typed edges encode support, requirement, inhibition, equivalence, and contradiction relations. Given a query and KG-acquired evidence, PG-LLM retrieves a query-specific PG context and uses its proposition content, graph structure, and typed relations to retain, qualify, reorganize, or suppress KG evidence. The PG context informs the refinement process but is not directly supplied to the answer generator, which receives the reorganized KG-derived evidence. We evaluate PG-LLM by isolating the evidence-reasoning stage in four KG-enabled reasoning pipelines while preserving their original KG-access mechanisms. Experiments on two medical benchmarks show consistent improvements in accuracy and safety-related outcomes, including an average accuracy gain of 7.84 percentage points (median +3.91) and a 24.18-percentage-point reduction in the Unsafe rate, while using substantially fewer evidence units. These results provide evidence that explicit applicability-aware evidence reasoning can improve the reliability of KG-enabled pipelines in the evaluated medical settings.

  • AutoMind: Adaptive Knowledgeable Agent for Automated Data Science

    Yujie Luo, Yixin Ou, Jingsheng Zheng, Lanning Wei, Zhuoyun Yu, Shuofei Qiao, Jintian Zhang, Da Zheng, Yuren Mao, Yunjun Gao, Huajun Chen, Ningyu Zhang

    Abstract

    Large Language Model (LLM) agents have shown great potential in addressing real-world data science problems. LLM-driven data science agents promise to automate the entire machine learning pipeline, yet their real-world effectiveness remains limited. Existing frameworks depend on rigid, pre-defined workflows and inflexible coding strategies; consequently, they excel only on relatively simple, classical problems and fail to capture the empirical expertise that human practitioners bring to complex, innovative tasks. In this work, we introduce AutoMind, an adaptive, knowledgeable LLM-agent framework that overcomes these deficiencies through three key advances: (1) a curated expert knowledge base that grounds the agent in domain expert knowledge, (2) an agentic knowledgeable tree search algorithm that strategically explores possible solutions, and (3) a self-adaptive coding strategy that dynamically tailors code generation to task complexity. Evaluations on two automated data science benchmarks demonstrate that AutoMind delivers superior performance versus state-of-the-art baselines. Additional analyses confirm favorable effectiveness, efficiency, and qualitative solution quality, highlighting AutoMind as an efficient and robust step toward fully automated data science.

  • Autonomous Prompt Evolution Framework for Combinatorial Auctions

    Haihui Zang, Heng Song

    Abstract

    LLM agents in dynamic competitive environments must both plan strategically and adapt from past interactions under substantial uncertainty. This paper studies combinatorial auctions, where agents bid under budget constraints while inferring hidden complement and substitute relations among items. Existing reflection and memory-augmented agents reuse cross-episode experience but summarize trajectories holistically, making it hard to identify which structural failure caused a missed bundle or redundant acquisition. We propose the Autonomous Prompt Evolution Framework (APEF), which treats the agent's natural-language intention as an evolvable policy optimized across episodes without weight updates, combining contrastive dual memory, in-context marginal-value simulation, and surprise-gated diagnostic evolution to convert synergistic failures and substitutable redundancies into executable prompt edits. On CABench, a synergy-aware extension of AucArena, APEF achieves the highest mean combinatorial profit across auction-order variation, randomized instances, backbone comparisons, and ablations, while maintaining low winner's-curse and no-acquisition rates—showing that structurally diagnosed prompt adaptation outperforms holistic reflection alone.

  • Beyond Retrieval Failure: Understanding Why LLMs Miss Evidence in Multi-Hop RAG

    Jiaqi Wei, Wenxuan Huang, Chengdong Xu, Xiang Zhang, Wanli Ouyang

    Abstract

    Multi-hop retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) requires an agent to retrieve evidence for a sequence of dependent sub-questions. When a wrong answer lacks a required passage, the passage may be inaccessible to the retriever, or the agent may have failed to issue a query that would retrieve it. Standard retrieval evaluations conflate these two causes. We study how much apparent retrieval failure is instead caused by search drift: the search no longer tracks an unanswered sub-question. Holding the retriever, corpus, and generation model fixed, we introduce \methodname, a training-free requirement register that records open sub-questions and their supporting evidence. The register changes search guidance but not evidence access, so each gold passage it recovers certifies that the passage was reachable by the original retriever. On 1{,}000 MuSiQue questions, the number of wrong answers with unretrieved required evidence falls from 382 to 234 with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and from 383 to 274 with Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B. These counts correspond to 69% to 44% and 66% to 48% of each system's wrong answers, respectively. Crucially, evidence recovery does not produce a proportional reduction in total error: premature resolutions and abstentions increase as missing-evidence failures decrease. The central finding is therefore a shift in the remaining bottleneck, from guiding search toward required evidence to verifying that the retrieved evidence is sufficient.

  • Budgeted Guidance Refresh Control: A Paired Throughput--Call Study in Lifelong Multi-Agent Path Finding

    Xiaoxiao Ma, Quan Fang, Xiankun Jiang

    Abstract

    Lifelong multi-agent path finding (LMAPF) requires robot fleets to serve a continuous stream of goals while maintaining high throughput under changing traffic conditions. Learned global guidance can improve coordination, but existing systems commonly refresh guidance at fixed intervals: frequent refresh wastes generator calls when demand is stable, whereas infrequent refresh can leave guidance stale after workload shifts. We formulate refresh timing as an independent lifecycle-control problem, operationalizing resource use as the number of logical guidance-generator calls, and instantiate it with Context-Aware Refresh and Recall (CARR). Using only online signals—demand change, context similarity, guidance age, and route adoption—CARR chooses among retaining the active guidance, recalling a cached guidance version, and generating a new one. It wraps a frozen CNN guidance generator and a frozen GPIBT planner without retraining either component, isolating the effect of refresh control. We evaluate eight refresh policies on stationary and non-stationary warehouse workloads using paired, policy-independent task streams and 3,840 runs organized into 40 independent root clusters. The 26-call periodic reference (Exact-B25) retains a small mean-throughput advantage over CARR (CARR effect: −0.80%; 95% CI: [−1.19%, −0.40%]), so the prespecified 1% non-inferiority objective is not established. Nevertheless, CARR uses approximately 79% fewer calls, forms a non-dominated sample-mean throughput–call operating point, and significantly outperforms all five evaluated low-call comparators; historical recall primarily substitutes for fresh generations rather than improving throughput. These results identify guidance refresh timing as a distinct systems-design dimension and provide a reproducible basis for selecting refresh policies within a fixed LMAPF guidance backbone under call constraints.

  • C-PAQ: A Counterfactual Provenance-Aware Quorum for Echo-Resistant Multi-Agent Fact Verification

    Lingdong Meng, Zeyu Song, Yu Guo

    Abstract

    Multi-agent large language model systems commonly aggregate agent judgments through majority voting, confidence weighting, or an LLM-based judge, yet these approaches often assume that agent reports are independent. In practice, communication can violate this assumption: downstream agents may copy or paraphrase an upstream conclusion without acquiring new evidence, causing the same information to be counted repeatedly and creating artificial corroboration. Distinguishing genuine support based on independent evidence from echo amplification caused by dependent communication, while ensuring that the final verdict covers all necessary evidence, remains a key challenge in multi-agent fact verification. To address this problem, we propose the Counterfactual Provenance-Aware Quorum (C-PAQ), a training-free aggregation mechanism. C-PAQ first performs a blind leave-one-source-out counterfactual audit to identify the evidence sources required for verifying a claim, then uses controller-recorded ancestry and direct source-access logs to contract dependent reports into evidence lineages. A claim is accepted as Supported only when the complete evidence packet passes verification, the required-source certificate is non-empty, and at least two distinct supporting lineages jointly cover all required sources. We evaluate C-PAQ on 120 matched complete and one-hop-missing evidence packets constructed from 60 HoVer claims with HotpotQA contexts, together with several controlled interventions. C-PAQ maintains an accuracy of 92.5% with both zero and seven dependent reports. In a conditioned stress test, seven descendants of an erroneous root reduce majority-voting accuracy from 100% to 0%, whereas C-PAQ retains 94.1% accuracy. Further experiments show that C-PAQ resists false support caused by copied citations and same-lineage re-access, while remaining responsive when an independent lineage acquires the missing evidence. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of C-PAQ in suppressing dependence-induced echo amplification and enforcing necessary-evidence coverage in multi-agent fact verification.

  • CACR: A Reconstructible Context Plane for Cloud-Native Agent Serving

    Wujian Huang, Xueyan Yang, Wenhai Wang, Yanting Zhang

    Abstract

    Most agent context architectures are local-first: a resident process owns the transcript, orders memory updates, and assembles each prompt. This design can work on a single host but does not by itself provide reliable context guarantees in cloud-native serving, where requests move across stateless replicas. Shared caches, indexes, and background writers also evolve independently, so model calls may observe stale, incomplete, or cross-tenant evidence. Existing memory and retrieval mechanisms optimize what to retain or retrieve but do not specify which evidence each cloud request may present to the model. We present the Cloud Agent Context Runtime (CACR), a context plane that expresses scope, authority, ordering, and evidence requirements as a request-level context contract. CACR stores scoped events and authorized artifacts as durable authority, treats recent windows, retrieval results, and compact state as replaceable projections, and deterministically selects among three context-construction paths. It also records requested and realized evidence, making degradation explicit. In Kubernetes and deployments on three Elastic Compute Service (ECS) instances, CACR preserves exact state across all 30 A–B–A dual-control rounds. It rejects foreign evidence in all 20,661 scope probes, even when tenant embeddings are identical. On LongMemEval-Oracle, every policy receives the same frozen, question-specific annotated evidence, system and task instructions, reader, and judge, isolating model-bound context construction from evidence discovery. Under this protocol, the CACR context pipeline achieves 93.6% accuracy, 7.2 and 11.8 percentage points above Always Recall and Full History, respectively. With the same prepared CACR evidence, open-weight 8B–27B readers achieve 83.6–86.2% accuracy. Under these workloads, CACR enforces context correctness at runtime without relying on process affinity.

  • Candidate Evidence Support Estimation and Routing from Single-Pass Generation Observations

    Shiyu Zhu, Hao Chen, Yichen Liu, Tang Shouyuan

    Abstract

    After candidate retrieval, a long-term memory system must decide which evidence windows fit within a limited generation context. Lexical or semantic similarity does not directly describe the generator's process state while using a candidate. We study candidate evidence-coverage readout between semantic-memory formation and final generation. For each question–candidate pair, a frozen model performs one deterministic trial answer; 20-dimensional predictive-entropy trajectories are extracted from the full vocabulary distribution and 22-dimensional stage statistics from attention allocations. An L2L_2 logistic regression forms the 42-dimensional Candidate Evidence Support Readout (CESR-42). On LoCoMo test candidates, CESR-42 obtains 0.8807 AUROC and 0.6680 AUPRC, exceeding the strongest scalar baseline, Sum NLL, at 0.7743 and 0.3300. On 351 test questions from four standard long-term-memory QA categories, it achieves 0.9164 Recall@5, 0.8871 MRR@5, and 0.8659 nDCG@5, with all point estimates above BM25's 0.9091, 0.8555, and 0.8429. Downstream answer metrics show different changes; in MultiHop, high evidence-identifier coverage does not guarantee nonzero answer-overlap scores. We position single-pass generation observations as an auditable and composable candidate-evidence routing interface between semantic memory and the final generator.

    Abstract

    Intelligent warehouse systems have transformed logistics by integrating robots with minimal infrastructure changes, boosting efficiency and optimizing workflows compared to traditional human-only operations.   • ChronoMem: When Relevance Is Not Validity in Long-Term Assistant Memory

    Yujie Shen

    Abstract

    Long-term memory is becoming a practical component of language-model assistants, agent runtimes, and personalized interactive systems. Yet a remembered preference is not simply a fact to retrieve. A user may replace it, make a temporary exception, scope it to work or personal use, or delete it. A memory can therefore be semantically relevant to a later query while being invalid for the current time and context. We introduce ChronoMem, a controlled benchmark for evaluating whether assistant-memory policies select the currently valid preference after temporally scoped memory events. The benchmark contains 360 auditable event sequences spanning 12 preference domains, five event types, and five temporal lags. We compare a transparent rule-based temporal filter, ChronoGate, with full-history prompting, recency, keyword retrieval, TF-IDF retrieval, time decay, and an Ollama dense-retrieval baseline across DeepSeek R1 8B, Qwen 2.5 7B, and Gemma 3 4B. The story is deliberately two-sided. On the original controlled benchmark, ChronoGate is strongest for both full-matrix models, reaching 1.000 accuracy with Qwen and 0.406 with DeepSeek, with positive paired-bootstrap intervals against every evaluated baseline. A harder lexical held-out split changes the conclusion: ChronoGate drops to 0.350, 0.708, and 0.722 on DeepSeek, Qwen, and Gemma, while full history or dense retrieval often matches or exceeds it. These results support ChronoMem as a useful benchmark for temporal validity in agent memory, but they also show that lexical heuristics are not a sufficient basis for general long-term memory systems.

  • Chronos-Lite: Causality-Aware Message Integration for Order-Robust LLM Agent Teams

    Chenxu Zhu, Zhiran Wang

    Abstract

    Parallel LLM agents can produce related updates under asynchronous execution, so message arrival order need not reflect information order. An integration policy based on arrival order may therefore allow a delayed ancestor to overwrite a causally newer revision. We formalize this failure as stale-message overwrite and introduce Chronos-Lite, a deterministic message-integration protocol that records parent dependencies and vector timestamps, maintains a maximal causal frontier, rejects causally dominated messages, and reconciles incomparable messages using a permutation-invariant rule. Under trusted causal metadata, Chronos-Lite satisfies a stale non-overwrite property; this structural guarantee does not imply semantic correctness. We evaluate eight integration methods using fixed cached outputs from heterogeneous DeepSeek and Qwen agents on 100 GSM8K and 100 ARC-Challenge tasks. Controlled replay covers 20,000 delivery schedules and 160,000 method traces, with paired bootstrap inference performed at the level of the 200 independent tasks. Across a balanced mixture of natural and diagnostic stress schedules, Chronos-Lite attains 94.51% accuracy compared with 91.79% for arrival-order last-write-wins, a difference of 2.72 percentage points with a 95% confidence interval of [1.15, 4.05]. It also reduces order sensitivity from 3.87% to 0.04% and correct-to-wrong transitions from 5.31% to 1.00%. The mean-accuracy difference is small under natural schedules, 94.50% versus 94.21%, and larger under targeted stale-message stress, 94.52% versus 89.36%. Lamport reaches 95.48% accuracy, so the current evidence does not support a mean-accuracy advantage for Chronos-Lite over a scalar total-order baseline. The guarantees are stale non-overwrite and same-set permutation invariance; the measured mixed-schedule robustness also reflects omissions. Final correctness remains dependent on the semantic resolver and the underlying agents.

  • Co-opetition Equilibrium in Adversarial Team Games with Heterogeneous Utilities

    Youzhi Zhang

    Abstract

    The United Nations' 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development requires all countries to collaborate against adversarial factors, a scenario that can be formalized as an adversarial team game. However, existing solution concepts assume team players share identical utility functions—an assumption inconsistent with real-world settings where countries have divergent objectives. This paper argues that studying adversarial team games must account for heterogeneous utilities among team players. We show that ignoring utility differences can render computed equilibria unstable in the original game, and we formalize this degradation via the Price of Ignoring Heterogeneity, which can be unbounded. To address this, we introduce the Co-opetition Equilibrium (CoE), where team players with heterogeneous utilities correlate their strategies (cooperation) against an adversary (competition). We establish existence via reduction from Nash equilibrium, and prove that finding a CoE is PPAD-complete while computing a Team-Maximizing CoE (TMCoE) is NP-hard. Nevertheless, we identify a broad class of zero-sum adversarial team games—those satisfying a consistent-constraint condition that generalizes identical utilities—where TMCoEs are exchangeable and computable in polynomial time via linear programming. We outline directions for algorithm design, MARL, and extensions to extensive-form and multi-team games.

  • Collab-Solver: Collaborative Solving Policy Learning for Mixed-Integer Linear Programming

    Siyuan Li, Yifan Yu, Zhihao Zhang, Mengjing Chen, Fangzhou Zhu, Tao Zhong, Jianye Hao, Peng Liu

    Abstract

    Mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) is a fundamental problem in combinatorial optimization, and conventional MILP solving primarily relies on carefully designed heuristics within the branch-and-bound framework. Driven by the strong capabilities of neural networks, recent research is exploring machine learning techniques alongside conventional MILP solving. Although learning-based MILP methods have shown great promise, existing work typically learns policies for individual modules in MILP solvers in isolation, without considering their interdependency, which limits both solving efficiency and solution quality.   • CoMAT A Collaborative Multi-Agent Framework for Black-Box Jailbreak Attacks via Attack-Trajectory Learning

    Fumin Cai, Jianing Li, Hao Li, Aimin Hao

    Abstract

    As large language models are widely deployed in conversational assistants, content generation, code development, and agentic systems, their safety-alignment mechanisms continue to face substantial challenges. Automated jailbreak attacks are an important tool for identifying safety weaknesses in models. However, existing white-box methods usually rely on internal model signals and are therefore sensitive to changes in model architecture and alignment strategies. Although black-box methods are more broadly applicable, they often require a new multi-round search for each incoming instruction, leading to high target-model query costs and external model invocation costs. To address these issues, this paper proposes a multi-agent black-box jailbreak attack framework for iterative optimization of attack instructions. The framework uses a teacher agent to collect successful and failed attack trajectories, converts successful trajectories into round-by-round supervised data for training a local attack agent, and thereby reuses historical instruction-transformation experience. Meanwhile, successful and failed candidates are used to train a screening agent, which filters low-potential instructions before querying the target model and reduces ineffective exploration. We conduct systematic evaluations on two safety benchmarks, HarmBench and AdvBench, against three closed-source models and three open-weight models.
