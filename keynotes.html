Michael I. Jordan

Inria Paris and the University of California, Berkeley

Member, National Academy of Sciences Member, National Academy of Engineering Member, American Academy of Arts and Sciences

Talk time: 1 December 2026 · 09:15–10:00

A Collectivist, Economic Perspective on AI

Abstract

Information technology is in the midst of a revolution in which omnipresent data collection and machine learning are impacting the human world as never before. The word "intelligence" is being used as a North Star for the development of this technology, with human cognition viewed as a baseline. This view neglects the fact that humans are social animals, and that much of our intelligence is social and cultural in origin. Thus, a broader framing is to consider the system level, where the agents in the system, be they computers or humans, are active, they are cooperative, and they wish to obtain value from their participation in learning-based systems. Agents may supply data and other resources to the system only if it is in their interest to do so, and they may be honest and cooperative only if it is in their interest to do so. Critically, intelligence inheres as much in the overall system as it does in individual agents. In the overall system, computation of equilibria replaces computation of optima. This is a perspective that is familiar in economics, although without the focus on learning algorithms. A key challenge for technology is thus to bring (micro)economic concepts into contact with foundational issues in the computing, statistical, and decision-making sciences. I'll discuss some concrete examples of problems and solutions at this tripartite interface.

Biography

Michael I. Jordan is a researcher at Inria Paris and Professor Emeritus at the University of California, Berkeley. His research interests bridge the computational, statistical, cognitive, biological and social sciences. Prof. Jordan is a member of the National Academy of Sciences, a member of the National Academy of Engineering, a member of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, a Foreign Member of the Royal Society, and a Foreign Member of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. He was a winner of a BBVA Foundation Frontiers of Knowledge Award in 2025 and was the inaugural winner of the World Laureates Association (WLA) Prize in 2022. He was a Plenary Lecturer at the International Congress of Mathematicians in 2018. He has received the Ulf Grenander Prize from the American Mathematical Society, the IEEE John von Neumann Medal, the IJCAI Research Excellence Award, the David E. Rumelhart Prize, and the ACM/AAAI Allen Newell Award.

Michael P. Wellman

Professor, University of Michigan

Talk time: 2 December 2026 · 09:15–10:00

Competition and Cooperation in LLM Bargaining: An Empirical Meta-Game Analysis

Abstract

Machine learning algorithms and LLM agents with interactive capabilities can be viewed as meta-strategies, mapping environment descriptions and training experience to strategies for interacting in particular situations. I present a framework that evaluates such techniques through induction of an empirical game model from simulation data. The approach is illustrated in the setting of two-player bargaining, applied to multiagent reinforcement learning algorithms as well as LLM agents. The analysis helps illuminate the potential tension between competition and cooperation in a canonical multiagent interaction setting.

Biography

Michael P. Wellman is Lynn A. Conway Collegiate Professor of Computer Science & Engineering at the University of Michigan. He received a PhD from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1988 for his work in qualitative probabilistic reasoning and decision-theoretic planning. From 1988 to 1992, Wellman conducted research in these areas at the USAF’s Wright Laboratory.

For the past 30+ years, his research has focused on computational market mechanisms and game-theoretic reasoning methods, with applications in electronic commerce, finance, and cyber-security. As Chief Market Technologist for TradingDynamics, Inc., he designed configurable auction technology for dynamic business-to-business commerce. Wellman previously served as Chair of the ACM Special Interest Group on Electronic Commerce (SIGecom), and as Executive Editor of the Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research. He is a Fellow of the Association for the Advancement of Artificial Intelligence and the Association for Computing Machinery.
